### Difference-in-Differences

### Difference-in-Differences (DiD) is a statistical method used to measure the true causal effect of a policy, tax, or event by comparing how outcomes change over time in a group affected by the policy (Treatment Group) against a similar group that was not affected (Control Group).
### Instead of simply looking at "before and after" in one place, DiD subtracts out background trends—such as inflation, seasonal shifts, or broader economic changes—that would have happened anyway.
### How It Works: The Two Differences
### Think of DiD as taking two steps to isolate the true impact of a change:
### •	First Difference (Over Time): You measure the change from before the policy to after the policy in the Treatment Group and do the exact same thing for the Control Group.
### •	Second Difference (Between Groups): You subtract the Control Group's change from the Treatment Group's change. Whatever is left over is the true effect of the policy.
